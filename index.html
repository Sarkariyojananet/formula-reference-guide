1. Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) Future Value Try Interactive SIP Calculator →

A Systematic Investment Plan compounds each monthly installment at the beginning of the period (annuity due). Unlike a single lump-sum deposit, every monthly contribution compounds for one fewer month than the previous contribution.

FV = P × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] × (1 + r)
  • P: Fixed monthly contribution
  • r: Monthly compounding rate = (Annual Expected Return % / 12) / 100
  • n: Total number of monthly installments = Years × 12

Worked Step-by-Step Example

Suppose you invest 5,000 per month for 10 years (n = 120) at an expected annual return of 12% (r = 0.01):

(1 + 0.01)^120 = 3.300387
FV = 5000 × [(3.300387 - 1) / 0.01] × 1.01 = 11,61,695
Common Mistake: Dividing annual rate by 12 works for standard mutual fund illustrations, whereas effective annual yield conversions use (1 + R)^(1/12) - 1. Always check which convention a financial institution uses.

2. Loan Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) Try Interactive EMI Calculator →

Reducing-balance loans (home, auto, and personal loans) charge interest only on the remaining unpaid principal each month. Early installments pay mostly interest; later installments pay mostly principal.

EMI = [P × R × (1 + R)^N] / [(1 + R)^N - 1]
  • P: Loan principal amount
  • R: Monthly interest rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100)
  • N: Total tenure in months

JavaScript Implementation

function calculateEMI(principal, annualRatePct, months) {
  if (annualRatePct === 0) return principal / months;
  const r = annualRatePct / 12 / 100;
  const factor = Math.pow(1 + r, months);
  return (principal * r * factor) / (factor - 1);
}

Notice the zero-interest guard (annualRatePct === 0) to avoid division by zero in floating-point arithmetic.

3. Concrete Volume & Dry Mix Estimation Try Concrete Calculator →

When dry cement, fine aggregate (sand), and coarse aggregate are mixed with water, finer particles fill the voids between coarse stones. Therefore, 1 m³ of wet compacted concrete requires approximately 1.54 m³ of dry loose materials (a 54% bulking/void allowance).

Dry Volume = Wet Volume × 1.54

Worked Example (1 m³ of M20 Concrete, Ratio 1 : 1.5 : 3)

  • Sum of ratio parts: 1 + 1.5 + 3 = 5.5
  • Cement volume: (1 / 5.5) × 1.54 = 0.28 m³ (÷ 0.035 m³/bag ≈ 8 bags of 50 kg)
  • Sand volume: (1.5 / 5.5) × 1.54 = 0.42 m³
  • Aggregate volume: (3 / 5.5) × 1.54 = 0.84 m³

4. Reinforcement Steel Unit Weight (IS 1786) Try RCC Beam Steel Calculator →

The unit weight of circular steel rebar is derived from the volume of a 1-meter cylinder multiplied by the standard density of mild/TMT steel (7,850 kg/m³):

Weight/m = (π / 4) × (D / 1000)² × 1 × 7850 = D² / 162.2

5. Percentage & Relative Percentage Change Try Percentage Calculator →

Percentage normalizes a ratio to a base of 100, while percentage change measures directional growth or decline relative to an initial baseline value.

Percentage (%) = (Part / Whole) × 100
Percentage Change = ((New Value - Old Value) / |Old Value|) × 100

Note the distinction between percentage change and percentage difference: percentage difference divides by the average of the two numbers (|A - B| / ((A + B) / 2)) × 100 because neither value is designated as the baseline.

6. Body Mass Index (BMI) Try BMI Calculator →

Body Mass Index is a simple population-level screening metric proposed by Adolphe Quetelet. Because mass scales roughly with height squared in adult proportions, BMI uses height² in meters.

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²
  • Underweight: < 18.5 kg/m²
  • Normal range: 18.5 – 24.9 kg/m²
  • Overweight: 25.0 – 29.9 kg/m²
  • Obese: ≥ 30.0 kg/m²
Limitation: BMI does not distinguish lean muscle mass from body fat, so athletes and older adults should interpret it alongside waist-to-height ratio.
